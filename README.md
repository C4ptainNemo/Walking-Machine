# Walking-Machine
A walking machine designed and 3D printed for a university project.

![Walking Machine](Pictures/Walking%20Machine.jpg)

The walking machine was part of learning about linkages, with this walking machine specifically being a two mirrored 4-bar linkages.

All parts were modeled and assembled in SolidWorks including the supplied motor and battery holder to validate the design prior to any printing of parts. The left and right-hand side linkages are mirrors of each other. The only parts that need to be mirrored for printing are the coupler/leg links and the rear input crank.

The parts were designed to be as light as possible to reduce the torque required from the motor. The motor was retained between the body and the battery holder using M2 bolts screwed into risers that were part of the body. The joints were designed for M2 bolts and used bushings so that the bolts could not be over tightened and prevent the links from rotating freely. The design of the input cranks enabled easy adjustment of the angle between them, allowing the timing of the front and rear feet to be set 180° apart.

The mounting of the input cranks made for easy install/uninstall the legs to the body. It also made offsetting the timing of the legs on the left and right side simple, by installing their respective cranks on the shaft 180° apart.
